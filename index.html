About

I'm a mechanical engineer with an M.S. from Virginia Tech, where I worked in the Soft Materials and Structures Lab at the junction of soft materials, electronics, and robot control. My thesis put a soft capacitive tactile sensor on a Franka FR3 and used its force feedback to pick up things a vision-only pipeline crushes — ripe tomatoes, strawberry slices.

What I enjoy most is the full stack of making a robot move well: mechanisms and actuators, sensor physics, PCB bring-up, firmware, and the control loops on top. I'm currently looking for roles in humanoid robotics and dexterous manipulation — end-effectors, tactile sensing, and the hardware–software seam between them.

Featured work

Feel before force: soft capacitive e‑skin for fragile‑object grasping

M.S. THESIS · SOFT MATERIALS AND STRUCTURES LAB, VIRGINIA TECH · 2026

Vision tells a robot where an object is. It says almost nothing about how hard the robot is squeezing it. This work adds the missing sense: a seven-layer soft capacitive sensor — silicone dielectrics, liquid-metal composite electrodes, and a plasma-treated PTFE electret — laminated onto the gripper and read out by a capacitance-to-digital converter on a custom PCB.

On a Franka FR3 running a vision-based grasping pipeline, the sensor closes the loop: contact is detected with motion-gated thresholding that rejects EMI from the moving arm, drift is handled with adaptive baseline correction, and every object class gets its own force threshold. Grasp success on fragile produce went from 0% under a joint-torque baseline to 100% with tactile feedback.

Projects

Selected builds

Hardware built outside the thesis — from a humanoid leg on my bench to a solar car that ran the 2024 American Solar Challenge.

2025 — present

4-DOF humanoid leg — BLDC, custom cycloidal drive, CAN

Designed and 3D-printed a four-degree-of-freedom leg driven by BLDC motors on ODrive S1 FOC controllers over CAN. Built a custom 8:1 cycloidal gearbox at ~70% torque efficiency with high backdrivability, tuned the position, velocity, and torque loops for precise joint-level trajectory tracking. First joint of a fully 3D-printed humanoid.

2021 — 2024

Solar Car at VT — suspension, concept to competition

Co-founded the university's solar car team and led the suspension from concept to production: SolidWorks design, ANSYS FEA to optimize geometry, then hands-on machining and assembly. The car passed every scrutineering phase and finished 12th at the 2024 American Solar Challenge, qualifying through the Formula Sun Grand Prix.
